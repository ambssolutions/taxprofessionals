How property can be held

One rentalMany people hold a first rental in their own name, or jointly with a partner. The rent and costs then go in your own income tax return each year. We prepare that return and help you keep the records it needs.

Several rentalsWith more than one rental, owners often keep holding them personally, or hold some through a company such as a look-through company (LTC). Each option treats income, losses and ownership differently. We look at what you have now and explain the options.

Building a portfolioAs a portfolio grows, some investors hold properties in a company or LTC, sometimes with a family trust as the shareholder. The right shape depends on your plans, your lending and your family. We help you weigh it up before anything changes.

Setting up a trustA family trust can own property, with trustees managing it for the beneficiaries. A trust keeps its own records and files its own income tax return. We help set up the trust's accounts and look after trust administration each year.

General information, not advice for your situation. Talk to us before you change a structure.

Questions property investors ask

Can you look at how my properties are held now?

Yes. Advice on property structures is one of our services, and the first consultation is free on all matters. Bring what you have and we will explain how things are set up now and what your options are. We talk any change through with you before anything is done.

Do you prepare returns for trusts and companies?

Yes. We prepare income tax returns for individuals, companies, trusts and partnerships, rental property annual returns, and we offer trust formation and management.

I bought to hold. Can the property still be taxed when I sell?

Possibly. The associated-persons rules can make a buy-and-hold property taxable when it is linked to development or trading. Every case is different, so talk to us before you buy or sell.

Do you work with tax pooling?

Yes, through Tax Management NZ (TMNZ), so you can pay provisional tax when it suits you. Ask us whether it makes sense for you.

Is the structure diagram advice for me?

No. It shows common ways property is held, in general terms only. Every situation is different. Talk to us before you change a structure.
